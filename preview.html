Jefferson County
Library, four ways.

A design study responding to jeffcolib.org — three totally different layout languages, each addressing the same core problems: burying the search, hiding the library card, and no clear "my account" affordance. Open any variation to see it live.

One brand system, four expressions.

All variations share the JCL palette (navy, red, orange, lilac, ink) and typography (Arimo + Comfortaa). What changes between them is composition, spacing, radius, shadow, and hierarchy — never the tokens.

What every variation fixes about jeffcolib.org

Problems observed

  • Search is small & buriedCatalog search sits in a corner; site search is separate — confusing for anyone who just wants "a book about X."
  • "Get a library card" is hiddenBuried in the services menu — the single highest-value CTA for a public library is invisible above the fold.
  • No obvious "My Account"Sign-in is small text; there's no dashboard showing holds, due dates, or fines at a glance.
  • Event calendar behind clicksHomepage teases a few events but the calendar itself lives on a separate page with dated filtering UI.
  • Weak mobileDesktop layout collapses awkwardly; the search box shrinks and CTAs stack in the wrong order.
  • No sense of "today"Static content — no acknowledgement of open branches, live event count, or personalization for signed-in users.

How each variation solves them

  • Search is the heroAll three make search the single largest interactive element on the page, with scope tabs (Catalog / Events / Research / Website).
  • Card CTA is a persistent actionCard button lives in the header on every variation and gets a dedicated hero-level section further down.
  • Account gets its own dashboardSigned-in users see their due dates, holds, and reading history right on the homepage — no clicks to check.
  • Live event calendar on the homepageFull month view (v2), sticky mini-calendar (v1), and week-strip with counts (v3). Not a teaser — the real thing.
  • Mobile-first responsiveAll variations tested at 640px and below; nothing critical hides on small screens.
  • Real-time signals"Open until 9 PM," "7 events today," "3 items due Friday" — the site knows what day it is and what's on right now.
